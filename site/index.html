Two Studio Displays. One volume.

A tiny menu bar app that plays your two Studio Displays as one stereo pair, gives you back the volume keys, keeps both speakers at the same level, and adds an equalizer.

Try it: press the keys, move the sliders, or wake the displays from sleep.

macOS can play to both. It just won't let you turn it down.

To use two Studio Displays as left and right speakers, you combine them into a Multi-Output Device. The moment you do, the volume keys and the menu bar volume control stop working, because macOS doesn't control the volume of combined devices.

Display Sound sets each display's volume directly, so the keys work again and both speakers move together.

Volume keys, back

Volume up, down and mute work on both displays at once, with an on-screen indicator in the corner.

Set up in one click

Click Use both displays and the app combines them into one stereo output and selects it. No Audio MIDI Setup.

Levels that stay matched

The two displays sometimes come back from sleep at different volumes. Display Sound notices and lowers the louder one. It never turns anything up on its own.

A shared equalizer, no driver

Five bands from bass to treble, ±12 dB. It uses macOS's built-in audio capture, so there's nothing extra to install, and audio never leaves your Mac.

Install

macOS 14.4 or laterApple silicon or IntelTwo Studio Displays
  1. Download and unzipMove Display Sound to your Applications folder.
  2. Allow it to openDisplay Sound isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks it the first time. Open it once, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Click Use both displaysThe app combines your displays into one stereo output and switches your Mac to it.
  4. Click Enable volume keysAllow Display Sound under Accessibility. That's what lets it see the volume keys.
